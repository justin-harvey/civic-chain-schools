CivicChain · K-12 spend control

See every card dollar at the register, not at the audit.

School districts run p-card programs through a bank: broad category blocks, monthly statements, and reconciliation to budget codes weeks after the money moved. CivicChain issues virtual cards bound to a budget code, enforces the merchant-category allowlist at authorization, and writes a redacted public record at settlement — so board reporting is a query, not a project.

This is a demonstration. Every figure is illustrative sample data. District names are archetypes, not real districts. Chart-of-account codes follow the structure of the Maine DOE handbook but are modeled for the demo, and the ledger anchoring / tamper-evidence is modeled. Nothing here is legal, financial, or audit advice.

How it works

Cards are scoped to budget codes in three tiers, funded from per-code pools, and policed at the authorization webhook.

Cards & pools

Three tiers — District, Building, Individual — each bound to a budget code (not a person) and drawing on that code's pool. Green chips are the merchant categories (MCCs) the card may use.

Authorization simulator

This is the control: pick a card, a merchant, and an amount, then run the authorization webhook. Out-of-policy categories and exhausted pools decline in real time, with a reason the cardholder sees.

Public ledger

Board reporting

Filter settled spend by building and date range and export it. Because each transaction was pre-coded to the chart of accounts at authorization, the export needs no manual coding.

Chart-of-accounts mapping

Every budget code resolves to a full COA string, so spend reconciles to the district's accounts automatically.
